**Canonical Corpus, Document 3 of 8 · Version 2.0 · June 2026**
**Satchel Hamilton · Civilodynamics Initiative**

> **Status and role.** This is the substantive theory of poliforms — what they are, why they exist, what evidence supports them, and how they cohere with the rest of Civilodynamics. The *formal* apparatus (the configuration space $\Omega$, the metric, basins of attraction, the dynamics, the transition taxonomy, and the two evidentiary tiers) is specified in **Document 2 (Mathematical & Methodological Foundations)**, to which this document defers; the *operationalization* of the constraint axes is in **Document 4 (Measurement Program)**. This document supplies the conceptual and empirical content those documents formalize and measure.
>
> **A note on this revision.** The poliform concept was developed after the core stress-dynamics machinery of Civilodynamics was already in place. A concept added late is at risk of being decorative — restated alongside the framework without doing real work in it. This version is written to remove that risk: §2 is devoted entirely to demonstrating that poliforms are *load-bearing* — that the discipline's central diagnostic does not function without them — and the empirical claims throughout are disciplined to the evidentiary tiers of Document 2 and graded by the strength of the underlying evidence.

## 1. Premise and definition

A **poliform** (Greek *polis*, civic body; Latin *forma*, form) is a recurrent, relatively stable configuration of social, political, economic, informational, and ecological organization that complex societies tend to converge upon under sufficiently similar constraint regimes. Formally (Document 2 §3), a poliform is a **basin of attraction** in the configuration space $\Omega$: a region toward which nearby configurations evolve and from which the system does not escape under small perturbation.

A poliform is not a civilization, a society, a regime, a polity, or a mode of production, though it intersects all of these. It is closer to a *bauplan* — a fundamental architectural pattern that constrains and enables a wide range of surface variation. Two societies sharing a poliform may differ enormously in language, religion, art, and ethnic composition while sharing the deep structure of how power flows, how surplus is extracted, how information moves, and how coordination scales. Poliforms are explicitly **not stages**: a society can move into, out of, between, and across them in either direction. They are attractors, not destinies.

The term was chosen over *civiloform* (etymologically clean but tonally clinical) and *stateform* (too narrow — it presumes the state, but pre-state, supra-state, and stateless poliforms exist). *Poliform* implies the political body without limiting itself to formal governance and admits the coinages the framework needs (sub-poliform, poliform transition, poliform morphospace, basin depth).

## 2. Why poliforms are load-bearing — coherence with the framework

This is the central section. The claim is not merely that poliforms are *compatible* with the rest of Civilodynamics, but that several of the discipline's core constructs do not work without them.

### 2.1 The CSI's central output is undefined without the basin map

The signature output of Civilodynamics is **distance-to-bifurcation** — how close a society is to qualitative reorganization (Document 2 §6.4). But distance to *what*? A distance requires a referent. The Civilizational Stress Index, taken alone, is an **anomaly score**: it measures how far a society's stress readout has departed from its own stable baseline. That tells you a society is unusual relative to its past; it does not, by itself, tell you that it is near a *transition*, nor what it would transition *into*.

Poliforms supply the missing referent. A basin boundary $\partial B$ is the surface a society must cross to leave its current poliform; distance-to-bifurcation is distance to that boundary, and the adjacent basins are the candidate destinations. The division of labor is exact:

- **The CSI supplies position and velocity** — where a society sits relative to its stable regime, and which way it is moving.
- **The poliform map supplies the terrain** — where the boundaries are, how deep the current basin is, and which attractors lie on the other side.

Neither is sufficient alone. A position with no map is an anomaly score; a map with no position is a static taxonomy. Together they are a diagnostic that can say: *this society is approaching the edge of the industrial-nation-state basin, with the algorithmic-surveillance basin as the nearest adjacent attractor, at this estimated rate.* That statement — a claim about the *kind* of transition in prospect — is the discipline's distinctive predictive product, and it is impossible without poliforms. This is the sense in which the concept is load-bearing rather than decorative.

### 2.2 The two state descriptions are two views of one object

Civilodynamics carries two vectors (Document 2 §1): the slow **structural configuration vector** $\mathbf{S} = (E, C, I, X, T, K)$ that locates a society in poliform morphospace, and the faster **stress readout** $\mathbf{u} = (\sigma_{\mathrm{econ}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{elite}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{leg}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{coh}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}, \sigma_{\mathrm{dem}})$ behind the CSI. Far from being unrelated, several stress observables are precisely the **fast deviations of the configuration axes from baseline**:

| Stress observable | Is, in part, the baseline-deviation of … |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{econ}}$ (economic stress) | the extraction axis $X$ (inequality, fiscal capacity, rent share) |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{leg}}$ (legitimacy) | the trust axis $T$ (institutional trust) |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}$ (epistemic coherence) | the information axis $I$ (the ECI is the operational reading of $I$) |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{coh}}$ (cohesion) | the trust axis $T$ (trust topology, sorting) |

This makes the relationship $\mathbf{u} = h(\mathbf{S}, \dot{\mathbf{S}}; \mathcal{B})$ of Document 2 concrete: stress is, substantially, the configuration drifting away from its stable values. It also flags a measurement obligation carried in Document 4 — the partial overlap between $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ must not be double-counted. Conceptually, the payoff is unifying: the poliform layer (where is this society, structurally?) and the stress layer (how far from stable is it?) are the same dynamical object read at two timescales.

### 2.3 Secular cycles live inside basins; poliform transitions cross between them

Turchin's secular cycles — the oscillations in elite overproduction, immiseration, fiscal capacity, and legitimation that the cliodynamics literature documents — correspond to the **rotational component** $R$ of the dynamics (Document 2 §6.1): motion *within* a basin. A society in the throes of a Goldstone–Turchin secular cycle moves substantially within its poliform without leaving it. Poliform **transitions** are the **cross-basin** events (Document 2 §6.3). This yields a clean and non-competitive division of labor with cliodynamics:

- **Cliodynamics describes the within-basin oscillation** — the secular cycle.
- **Poliform theory describes the basin structure and the between-basin transitions.**

The contemporary United States is, on this reading, simultaneously at a secular-cycle stress peak (an intra-basin "Age of Discord") *and* possibly in the early phase of a between-basin transition (industrial nation-state → algorithmic surveillance state). Distinguishing these two — a within-basin peak that will relax versus the onset of a basin crossing — is one of the sharpest questions the framework poses, and Document 5 treats it as such.

### 2.4 Timescale reconciliation — why high stress is not imminent collapse

Poliform transitions are slow (often centuries); the anti-systemic dynamics of the U.S. case are fast (years). These are reconciled by **basin depth**. A society can sit in the high-stress zone *near* a boundary for a long time without crossing it, because crossing requires either sustained endogenous drift to the boundary or a shock large enough to overcome the basin's remaining depth (Document 2 §6). Therefore elevated CSI means *elevated transition probability over a horizon* — a society in the storm-generative zone — **not** imminent reorganization. This both coheres with the dynamics and disciplines the prediction claims: a high CSI is a statement about probability and proximity, never a forecast of a dated collapse.

### 2.5 The information axis makes poliforms an expression of the discipline's distinctive claim

Civilodynamics' central novelty is the treatment of the information/epistemic layer as a first-class state variable (Documents 1, 2). Poliforms are not separate from this claim; they express it. The information-architecture axis $I$ is one of the primary axes that *individuate* large-scale poliforms — scribal-vertical, print-lateral, broadcast, and algorithmic information regimes sustain structurally different societies — and the newest candidate poliform (the algorithmic surveillance state) is defined precisely by its information architecture. A change in the information axis is, in poliform terms, a **basin-reshaping** event (Document 2 §6.3, Type 2). Poliform theory thus inherits and extends the discipline's distinctive lens rather than standing apart from it.

## 3. The empirical foundation and its limits

Poliform theory would be speculative if it stood alone. It does not — but its evidentiary status must be stated precisely, because the strength of the evidence varies by claim.

### 3.1 The convergent-clustering evidence

A substantial computational-social-science literature finds that human societies cluster into a small number of recurrent configurations. Peregrine (2018), applying morphospace analysis to comparative archaeological data, identified recurrent social formations — empirical clusters in social-ecological state space. Shults and colleagues (PLOS ONE, 2020) subjected the Seshat Global History Databank to multidimensional clustering and found a best-fit five-cluster solution nested within two superclusters, with statistically significant separation — conceptually replicating Peregrine using different data and a different method. Ullah, Kuijt, and Freeman (2015) modeled subsistence change as a punctuated process between attractor states; Atkinson and Whitehouse mapped a morphospace of ritual form. Different methods, different data, recurrently similar architecture.

Three features of this body of work matter. It is **explicitly non-teleological**: recurrent formations are not stages, and trajectories run in both directions. The morphospace is **lumpy, not smooth**: societies cluster densely in a few regions and are sparse to absent elsewhere — the empirical signature theoretical morphology predicts when constraints operate. And it is **scale-sensitive**: different poliforms dominate different population regimes, because the constraints themselves change with scale (a band of 200 cannot be an industrial state; 200 million cannot be a face-to-face band). The morphospace is a layered atlas, not a single map.

### 3.2 The critical limits — stated honestly

Three caveats discipline the claims, and they are not minor.

**Clusters are Tier 1; attractors are a Tier 2 hypothesis.** The evidence above establishes *clustering* — dense regions in configuration space — which is Tier-1 evidence (cross-sectional density; Document 2 §3, §6.2). Calling these clusters *attractors*, with basins and convergent dynamics, is the stronger Tier-2 claim that societies actually *move toward* and *stay in* these regions. Clustering is consistent with attractors but does not by itself prove them: a cluster could arise from convergent dynamics, from shared ancestry, from diffusion, or from how the data were partitioned. **The attractor interpretation is a hypothesis with a stated test** — confirm, on longitudinal trajectory data, that societies near a cluster move toward its center and resist small perturbations (Tier 2; Document 2 §7). Until that test is run, poliforms are *empirically attested clusters and hypothesized attractors*, and the corpus says exactly that.

**Convergence versus diffusion — the central confound.** Biological convergent evolution is compelling precisely because it occurs across *information-isolated lineages*: an Australian marsupial and a placental wolf cannot exchange genes, so their similarity must be independent rediscovery under shared constraint. Civilizations are **not** information-isolated — they trade, emulate, conquer, and copy. Apparent poliform convergence may therefore be **diffusion** (one form spreading) rather than independent rediscovery of an attractor. This is the single most important limit on the biology-to-civilization transfer, and the original framing underweighted it. The discipline's response is to **grade convergence claims by the independence of the cases** (§5): the strongest evidence comes from cases that were genuinely out of contact (the steppe-nomad confederacy realized independently in Eurasia and on the North American plains is the flagship example), and the weakest from cases dominated by diffusion (the industrial nation-state, which spread globally from a few origins).

**The unit-of-analysis problem.** Cluster results are sensitive to how the data are partitioned into "societies." Seshat's polity-based partitioning is one defensible choice among several, and it interacts with the nesting problem (§7). The framework owes an explicit theory of when two adjacent populations are one unit or two — and that same theory fixes the unit at which the configuration vector and the stress vector are both computed, so that poliform classification and CSI measurement refer to the same object.

## 4. The biology link: homology, not identity

The framework borrows from theoretical morphology — the branch of evolutionary biology that maps the geometric space of possible biological forms and asks why only a small subset is realized. McGhee's program demonstrated, for several lineages (ammonoid and brachiopod shells, helical bryozoan colonies), that the occupied region of possible-form-space is a small fraction of the geometrically possible region, the empty regions excluded by functional and developmental constraints. This is the structural intuition behind convergent evolution: complex systems under similar constraints converge on similar solutions because the space of viable solutions is small. Sabertooth morphologies, crab body plans, powered flight, and anteater forms each evolved independently several times.

> **Correction (vs. v1.0 of the poliform paper).** The earlier draft asserted the borrowing was "not analogy but structural identity." This is an overclaim and is retired. The correct relationship is **structural homology** — a *shared mathematical structure*, not an identity of substance. Precisely:
>
> **What is shared.** The mathematics of constraint-shaped morphospace: a vast space of conceivable configurations, a small realized subset carved by hard constraints, and convergence onto a finite library of forms. The geometry of attractor-shaped configuration space does not care whether the system is built of cells or institutions. This shared structure is real and is what licenses the transfer.
>
> **What is not shared.** (i) *Substrate* — bone strength and oxygen diffusion versus information flow, coordination scaling, energy extraction, trust mathematics, and carrying capacity. (ii) *The generative process* — in biology, development (genotype→phenotype); in civilizations, the constraint stack and its dynamics $\mathbf{F}$ generating feasible configurations. (iii) *The selection process* — in biology, differential survival and reproduction of organisms; in civilizations, the differential *persistence and replication of social forms*, a hypothesized cultural-institutional selection that is itself contested, not an established mechanism. (iv) *Lineage isolation* — biology's convergence is across information-isolated lineages; civilizational "convergence" is contaminated by diffusion (§3.2).
>
> Calling the relationship homology rather than identity is not a hedge; it is what makes the framework defensible to the biologists whose tools it borrows, and it foregrounds the diffusion confound that the identity framing obscured.

## 5. A provisional catalog of candidate poliforms

The catalog is a **first-pass, falsifiable taxonomy of candidate basins**, not a finished result. Boundaries are fuzzy, real societies combine elements of several, and the list is rough-ordered by the energy and population scale at which each is sustainable — with the explicit warning that ordering by scale invites the teleological misreading the framework rejects (§7). Each entry is graded by the strength of its convergence evidence, which (per §3.2) turns on how independent the realizing cases were.

| Poliform | Scale (pop.) | Defining coordination / information signature | Convergence evidence |
|----------|--------------|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------|
| Foraging band | 25–150 | Face-to-face; oral, lateral | Ancestral attractor (similarity partly by descent, not pure convergence) |
| Tribal confederacy | 10³–10⁴ | Segmentary kinship; oral + mnemonic | Strong (many independent origins) |
| Chiefdom | 5×10³–5×10⁴ | Ranked kinship, redistribution; oral + material symbols | Strong (≥8 independent origins) |
| Agrarian state | 5×10⁴–10⁷ | Scribal bureaucracy; scribal-vertical information | Strong origins (≥6 independent), heavy later diffusion |
| **Pastoral-nomadic confederacy** | 10⁵–10⁶ effective | Mobile kin hierarchy, charismatic-meritocratic leadership | **Strongest** (Eurasian steppe *and* North American plains — genuinely isolated) |
| City-state system | 10⁴–2×10⁵ per unit | Civic identity, deliberative councils; dense lateral exchange | Moderate–strong (several independent, some diffusion) |
| Trading thalassocracy | Variable | Mercantile oligarchy, naval power; maritime information networks | Moderate (much Mediterranean/European diffusion) |
| Universalist empire | 10⁷–10⁸ | Imperial bureaucracy, universalist ideology; scribal-vertical | Moderate–strong on *convergent pathologies* (succession, collapse modes) |
| Industrial nation-state | 5×10⁶–5×10⁸ | Mass parties, standing army; print-then-broadcast information | **Weak** as convergence (≈2 origins + massive diffusion) |
| Algorithmic surveillance state | Variable / platform-global | Platform-mediated governance; algorithmic real-time information | **Untested** (novel; recurrence across traditions may be diffusion) |

Niche and edge-case forms with smaller bibliographies — the theocratic federation, the trading diaspora, the charter colony, the revolutionary vanguard regime, the failed-state oligopoly (a genuine attractor, not merely an absence), and the corporate sovereign — extend the morphospace and are candidate basins in their own right.

Four entries deserve discussion because they bear on the discipline's central claims.

**The pastoral-nomadic confederacy is the catalog's evidentiary flagship.** The Scythians, Xiongnu, Mongols, and Turks of the Eurasian steppe and the Comanche and Plains Sioux of North America converged on structurally similar arrangements — mobile kin hierarchies, charismatic-meritocratic leadership selection, tribute-and-raid economies, rapid coalescence-fragmentation cycles — despite the New World cases being wholly out of contact with the Old. This is the closest thing the framework has to biology's information-isolated convergence, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence that poliforms are real attractors under shared ecological constraint rather than artifacts of diffusion.

**The universalist empire's convergence is in its dynamics, not just its form.** Achaemenid Persia, Han and Tang China, Rome, the Caliphates, the Mughals, and the Ottomans recur not only in structure but in their characteristic *failure modes* — succession crises, bureaucratic pathologies, similar collapse trajectories. Convergent dynamics across cases with mixed independence is itself suggestive of an attractor, because diffusion explains shared *structure* more easily than it explains shared *instability patterns*.

**The industrial nation-state is, candidly, the weakest convergence case — and it is the baseline for the flagship application.** It arose from roughly two independent pathways (a European and an East Asian) and then spread globally by diffusion, emulation, and coercion. Its "recurrence" is therefore mostly one diffusion event from a few origins, not independent rediscovery. This matters because the U.S. case study (Document 5) takes the industrial nation-state as the home basin whose stability is in question. The honest position is that the industrial nation-state's status as a *convergent attractor* is the least secure in the catalog, even as its status as the *dominant realized form* of the past two centuries is uncontested.

**The algorithmic surveillance state is a candidate new basin of unknown stability.** It is realized differently in China (state-held) and the U.S./West (privately held, decentralized, but functionally similar in information capture). Its recurrence across radically different political traditions within ~15 years is suggestive — but it could equally be **diffusion** of the same underlying technology rather than independent convergence, and it is far too new to have demonstrated the persistence that would make it an attractor. It is the central instance of the novelty-versus-recurrence problem (§7), and it is precisely the basin the contemporary transition is hypothesized to be moving toward — which makes its uncertain status a live concern for the discipline's most important present-day claim, not an academic footnote.

## 6. Transition dynamics

The formal taxonomy of transitions (cross-basin, basin-reshaping, bifurcation) and the roles of the potential, the rotational component, and the shock term are specified in Document 2 §6. Conceptually:

**Within-basin motion** is the secular cycle (§2.3): a society circulates through elite-overproduction and legitimation cycles without leaving its poliform.

**Between-basin transitions** are the poliform changes. Some are common (chiefdom → agrarian state, many independent times); some are rare or path-constrained (agrarian state → industrial nation-state, roughly twice); some have never occurred (foraging band directly to industrial state — intermediate poliforms appear to be necessary stepping stones, plausibly because the institutional scaffolding for each scale must be assembled in sequence).

**Collapse** is a downward transition: a rapid loss of complexity when the energy subsidy supporting it can no longer be maintained (Tainter) — in poliform terms, a transition from a higher-energy to a lower-energy attractor, often skipping intermediate forms (Western Rome, Classic Maya, Bronze Age Aegean, Ancestral Puebloan).

**Hysteresis** is real and underappreciated: returning to a prior poliform is not reversing the conditions that drove the original transition. A post-industrial society does not become an agrarian state by losing its industry; it becomes a new configuration with industrial ruins and vestigial institutions. The path back is not the path forward in reverse.

Some poliforms appear to be **local dead-ends**: the pastoral-nomadic confederacy reliably produces large empires that reliably fragment within a few generations; the city-state system reliably produces commercial florescence and reliably gets absorbed by a larger universalist empire when one is available. Whether the industrial nation-state has an analogous built-in instability is one of the framework's open questions, and the contemporary moment — simultaneous shifts across all six constraint axes — is best modeled as the approach to a multi-parameter bifurcation (Document 2 §6.3, Type 3) in which several attractors lose stability while one or more new ones form.

## 7. What poliforms make possible, and the open problems

### 7.1 What the framework gains from poliforms

**Retrodiction with content.** Because the CSI's distance-to-bifurcation acquires a referent from the basin map (§2.1), specific historical transitions become retrodictable not merely as "stress was high" but as "the society approached *this* boundary toward *that* adjacent attractor." This is the methodology of Document 6.

**A contemporary diagnostic.** The same apparatus, run on the present, identifies where the current global configuration is unstable and which adjacent attractors are within reach — the subject of a planned contemporary-diagnostic study.

**A demarcated exobiological extension.** If the morphospace is shaped by physical, informational, and ecological constraints rather than anything specifically human, the framework should generalize to any sufficiently complex social species, with different constraints (different cognitive architectures setting different Dunbar equivalents, different metabolisms setting different complexity envelopes) but the same geometry of constraint-shaped attractor space. This is stated as a **research hypothesis kept deliberately separate from the empirical core** (Document 1 scope; Document 2 §10) — intriguing, but not something the discipline's credibility should rest on.

### 7.2 The open problems — the agenda

These are not fatal flaws; they are the work, and each connects to a specific part of the program.

- **Unit of analysis and nesting.** What counts as one society, and how do poliforms nest (the poliform of New York City is not that of rural Mississippi within one nation-state)? The framework needs either a formal nesting structure (sub-poliforms within poliforms) or a probabilistic mixture model — and the resolution must fix the common unit at which both $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{u}$ are computed. *(Links to Document 4 §1, and to the partitioning sensitivity of §3.2.)*
- **Constraint quantification.** The six-layer constraint stack must be measurable for any society at any time for the morphospace to do predictive work. *(This is the configuration-vector measurement program of Document 4 §7; the information and trust axes in deep history are the priority gaps.)*
- **Attractor status (the Tier-2 test).** Confirm on longitudinal data that clusters behave as attractors, not merely as dense regions. *(Document 2 §7; Document 8.)*
- **Convergence versus diffusion.** Develop a method to separate independent attractor-filling from diffusion — minimally, by weighting the catalog's evidence toward information-isolated cases and testing whether contact history predicts configuration similarity. *(§3.2; a defined methodological task.)*
- **Novelty versus recurrence.** Distinguish a genuinely new attractor that has been entered from a transient that will resolve into an existing one — the live question for the algorithmic surveillance poliform. *(§5.)*
- **The teleology trap.** The catalog, ordered by scale, is easily misread as a hierarchy or a ladder of progress. The framework explicitly disclaims this, and the disclaimer must be carried into every presentation, because the pull of teleological framing in this kind of work is strong. *(A presentational commitment, not a technical one.)*

## 8. Closing

Poliform theory proposes that civilizational variety, like biological variety, is shaped by a constrained morphospace and converges on a small library of recurring attractors. The empirical literature supports the *clustering*; the attractor interpretation is a disciplined hypothesis with a stated test; the constraint stack supplies a candidate explanation for the geometry; and the catalog provides a working, falsifiable taxonomy, graded honestly by the independence of its cases.

But the deeper point of this revision is coherence. Poliforms are not a biological metaphor appended to a stress model. They are the **structural backbone** of the discipline: the basins whose boundaries give the Civilizational Stress Index its meaning, the terrain on which secular cycles and between-basin transitions divide the labor between cliodynamics and Civilodynamics, and the natural expression of the information-layer claim that distinguishes the discipline. The relationship to biology is homology — a shared mathematics of constraint-shaped attractor space — not identity, and certainly not mere metaphor. The constraints differ; the geometry is shared; and the diffusion confound that distinguishes the civilizational case from the biological one is named rather than hidden.

If the framework is right, the central questions of civilizational analysis stop being "why this society in this place at this time" and become "which attractor was reachable from which initial conditions under which constraint regime, and how close is a given society now to the boundary of the basin it occupies." Those are questions with answers. Finding them — and giving the Civilizational Stress Index the basin map it needs to mean anything — is what poliform theory is for.
