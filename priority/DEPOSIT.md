## 2. Abstracts (ready to paste)

### 2a. Corpus-level (Zenodo / OSF project description)

> Civilodynamics is the quantitative science of how civilizations maintain
> coherence, accumulate stress, approach phase transitions, and reorganize, with
> particular attention to the role of information architecture in accelerating or
> damping these dynamics. It extends — and does not replace — the cliodynamics
> program of Turchin and colleagues, staking its distinctness on three narrow
> claims: the epistemic/information layer as a first-class state variable; the
> integration of recurrent social forms with theoretical morphospace (the
> *poliform* framework); and real-time measurement. This corpus comprises a
> Foundational Charter, mathematical and methodological foundations, poliform
> theory, a measurement program and data architecture, a flagship U.S. case study
> with registered predictions, a retrodiction casebook and pre-registration, a
> lexicon and ontology, a living program map, and the first building-phase
> documents (the Factual Consensus Rate instrument, item bank, and pre-analysis
> plan). At this stage the framework is a coherent, falsifiable skeleton: no index
> has been computed end-to-end against data, and reported quantities are
> illustrative estimates, labeled as such. The work ahead is measurement,
> computation, and validation.

### 2b. Foundational Charter (#1) — arXiv abstract

> This is the constitutional document of Civilodynamics, a proposed quantitative
> science of civilizational dynamics. It states the discipline's four binding
> commitments (the civilization as unit of analysis; the information/epistemic
> layer as a first-class state variable; falsifiable probabilistic prediction; and
> phase transitions as the central predictive object), situates the program within
> a century and a half of attempts at a rigorous social science, and positions it
> as an extension of — not a competitor to — cliodynamics. It specifies the
> validation architecture (retrodiction bar, prospective Brier-scored prediction,
> explicit falsifiability conditions), the measurement commitment, the ethical
> commitments, and the institutional blueprint. All mathematics is deferred to the
> Mathematical and Methodological Foundations; the empirical application lives in a
> companion case study. The discipline's validation is, at this stage, promissory:
> the charter sets the principles under which the calibration and validation work
> proceeds.

### 2c. Flagship case study (#5) — arXiv abstract

> We apply Civilodynamics — a framework that models societies as complex dynamical
> systems and treats civilizational stress as the primary structural variable
> behind political outcomes — to the United States over 2000–2024. The framework
> represents a society's stress state as a vector across six blocks (economic,
> elite, legitimacy, cohesion, epistemic, demographic), reduced to a scalar
> Civilizational Stress Index. Using public-domain data, we present an illustrative
> stress trajectory and argue, as a falsifiable structural-primacy hypothesis, that
> the six stress blocks were jointly elevated and rising before the political
> developments of 2015–2016 that candidate-centered accounts treat as causes — so
> that anti-systemic political emergence is better read as a structurally probable
> output of a high-stress state than as a candidate-specific anomaly. Contested
> causal claims are demoted to operationalized, falsifiable sub-hypotheses; the
> Weimar comparison is framed as hypothesis-generating analogy, not validation. All
> index values are illustrative pending end-to-end computation; the substance under
> test is the qualitative structural account and five registered, Brier-scorable
> predictions for 2026–2030.
