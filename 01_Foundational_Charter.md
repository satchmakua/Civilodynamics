# Civilodynamics — Foundational Charter

**Canonical Corpus, Document 1 of 8 · Version 2.0.1 · June 2026, corrected November 2026**
**Satchel Hamilton · Civilodynamics Initiative**

> **Status and role.** This is the constitution of the discipline: it states why Civilodynamics exists, what it commits to, how it is organized, and what would prove it wrong. It supersedes the v1.0 Foundational Charter. It is deliberately *not* a technical document — all mathematics is specified in **Document 2 (Mathematical & Methodological Foundations)**, to which this charter defers throughout; the empirical application to the United States, which appeared in v1.0, now lives in **Document 5**; and the detailed, living research-and-dissemination program lives in **Document 8 (Program & Milestone Map)**. This charter sets the principles those documents operate under.

---

> *Civilodynamics is the quantitative science of how civilizations maintain coherence, accumulate stress, approach phase transitions, and reorganize — with particular attention to the role of information architecture in accelerating or damping these dynamics.*

---

## 1. The necessity of a new discipline

The twenty-first century has made the limits of the existing social sciences difficult to ignore. Within a single generation the global financial system approached collapse with little warning from its professional stewards; a series of consolidated democracies underwent political transformations their own institutional analysts rated as improbable; a pandemic exposed the brittleness of supply chains, public-health systems, and epistemic institutions at once; and algorithmic information systems reshaped the political psychology of whole populations faster than any regulatory or scholarly framework could track.

In retrospect, none of these were structurally unforeseeable. The conditions that produced them were, in each case, measurable in advance. What was missing was not data but a framework capable of integrating that data into a coherent picture of a civilization's structural state and trajectory. Economics studied the financial system near equilibrium. Political science studied elections and institutions at the level of events. Sociology described social structure without modeling its dynamics. History narrated the past without formalizing its regularities. Each illuminated a part while the whole moved toward crisis unobserved.

Civilodynamics exists to address that gap. It does not aspire to predict specific events — the ambition of point-prediction is precisely the failure mode of earlier grand theories. It aspires to something more modest and more achievable: to characterize the structural state of a civilization quantitatively, to identify when that state has entered a regime in which qualitative disruption becomes probable, and to track the evolution of that probability with calibrated rigor. In the language of meteorology, Civilodynamics does not forecast the specific storm; it measures when and where atmospheric conditions have become storm-generative.

## 2. What Civilodynamics is

Four commitments distinguish the discipline and define its identity. Each is binding; departing from any of them would make the work something other than Civilodynamics.

**The civilization is the unit of analysis.** The object of study is the dynamic structural state of the society as a whole — not its events, actors, or institutions in isolation, but the coupling between its subsystems and the emergent behavior that coupling produces.

**The information and epistemic layer is a first-class state variable.** The structural transformation of the information environment is treated not as background context but as a primary dynamic variable that alters the transfer function between structural stress and political outcome. This is the discipline's most distinctive commitment (§3, §5).

**Falsifiable probabilistic prediction is the standard of validity.** Following the superforecasting tradition, claims are expressed as probabilities over defined outcomes with stated resolution criteria, timestamped in advance, and scored. A discipline that cannot specify the conditions under which its claims would fail is not a science (§6).

**Phase transitions are the central predictive object.** The discipline targets moments of qualitative civilizational reorganization — the approach to bifurcation — rather than steady-state description. It predicts *regime type*, not specific events.

### Scope: what the discipline does and does not claim

These limits are stated up front, because honesty about them is itself a methodological commitment (the full statement is in Document 2 §10):

- It **does not predict specific events** — which war, election, or crisis, or when. It locates structural boundaries and estimates stability margins.
- It **does not eliminate contingency.** Determinism in the underlying dynamics coexists with genuine stochasticity in the shocks that perturb them. (This is the formal content of the *Mule Problem*, after Asimov's singular disruptor: the framework predicts that a Mule-type emergence becomes *likely* in sufficiently stressed states, never *which individual* fills the role.)
- It is, in its present form, **anthropocentric** — built on human cognition, metabolism, and ecology. Generalization beyond the human case is a research program kept deliberately separate from the empirical core.
- Its validation is, at this stage, **promissory.** No index has yet been computed end-to-end against data; no retrodiction has been scored. The framework is a coherent, falsifiable skeleton, and the calibration and validation that would earn it the name of science are the work ahead, not a footnote to it.

## 3. Intellectual lineage

Civilodynamics does not emerge from nothing. It stands on a century and a half of serious attempts to bring scientific rigor to the study of human societies. Each captured something real and fell short in a characteristic way; understanding those shortfalls is the argument for what this discipline must do differently.

**Auguste Comte** named sociology with the explicit ambition of a "social physics." The ambition was right; the execution lacked any mathematical formalism, measurement program, or falsifiability standard. **Marx** built a structural theory in which material conditions constrain ideological possibility — a lasting contribution — but rendered it teleological and deterministic, unable to accommodate contingency or the reflexive modification of outcomes by actors aware of the theory. **Talcott Parsons** achieved an abstraction so general it could describe everything and predict nothing. **E. O. Wilson's** sociobiology grounded individual and small-group behavior in evolution but could not bridge to civilization-scale emergent dynamics. The **Santa Fe Institute** complexity tradition supplied the right conceptual repertoire — self-organized criticality, emergence, power laws, phase transitions — but largely as analogy rather than literal structural description, without a unified measurement program at civilizational scale. **Sociophysics** (Galam, Stauffer, and successors) applied statistical-mechanics models with exactly the right tools, but mostly at the micro-to-meso scale of opinion dynamics. **Mainstream economics** achieved genuine predictive success in narrow market contexts on equilibrium assumptions that exclude, by construction, the non-equilibrium dynamics most relevant to transitions. **Political science** produced sophisticated event-level and comparative work while systematically failing to anticipate major structural disruptions, because the conditions that make a transition likely are largely independent of the specific event that triggers it. **Tetlock's** superforecasting established that calibrated probabilistic reasoning can be scored and can outperform expert intuition — the most epistemically serious tradition in the social sciences, and one Civilodynamics adopts wholesale — but it is a methodology without a theory, operating on short horizons and narrow questions.

**Cliodynamics and the work of Peter Turchin occupy a different place in this lineage, and the distinction must be stated plainly.** Cliodynamics is not one predecessor among many; it is the direct intellectual parent of Civilodynamics. The structural-demographic model, secular-cycle theory, the practice of timestamped falsifiable prediction, and the Seshat Global History Databank are genuine scientific achievements, and the quantitative-historical approach this discipline depends on would lack its empirical grounding without them. **Civilodynamics extends cliodynamics; it does not supersede or replace it, and it positions itself as a collaborator with that program rather than a competitor to it.** Its claim to be a distinct discipline rests on three — and only three — narrow extensions:

1. **The epistemic/information layer as a first-class state variable.** Cliodynamics models the information environment thinly; Civilodynamics treats it as a primary axis of the state space and as a structural accelerant of the translation from stress to outcome.
2. **The integration of recurrent social formations with theoretical morphospace.** Civilodynamics explicitly connects the empirical clustering found in the comparative record (Peregrine; the Seshat clustering of Miranda and Freeman) with the morphospace tradition in evolutionary biology, and reads the clusters as candidate attractors, yielding the **poliform** framework (Document 3).
3. **Real-time measurement.** Cliodynamics is calibrated primarily on historical data; Civilodynamics aims to fuse the historical baseline with contemporary, real-time measurement streams.

Everything else of substance the two share. Overstating the difference would be both inaccurate and strategically self-defeating; the discipline's posture toward Turchin and his collaborators is one of acknowledged debt and active alliance.

## 4. The theoretical core (in brief)

The formal apparatus is the subject of Document 2; what follows is a conceptual précis sufficient to orient the reader. Nothing here is derived — Document 2 derives it, and where this charter and that document might appear to differ, Document 2 governs.

**Two state descriptions.** A society is described at two coupled levels: a slow-moving **structural configuration vector** that locates it in the poliform morphospace along six constraint axes (energy, coordination, information, extraction, trust, ecology), and a faster-moving **stress readout** (economic, elite, legitimacy, cohesion, epistemic, demographic) that measures its distance from its own stable regime. The stress readout is a derived projection of the configuration and its rate of change relative to a defended baseline.

**Poliforms as attractors.** Human societies do not distribute evenly across the space of conceivable configurations; they cluster into a small library of recurrent forms. Civilodynamics interprets these as **basins of attraction** — the conceptual keystone that distinguishes the discipline from descriptive historiography. The relationship to biology is one of **structural homology**, a shared mathematical structure of constraint-shaped attractor spaces, not an identity of substance.

**Measures.** The **Civilizational Stress Index (CSI)** is a scalar reduction of the stress readout, measuring distance from the stable regime; its construction and the correct interpretation of that distance are specified in Document 2. The **Epistemic Coherence Index (ECI)** operationalizes the information axis and is the discipline's priority metrological build (§5).

**Dynamics and the central diagnostic.** The configuration evolves under a deterministic field plus stochastic shock; within a poliform it circulates through the secular cycles the cliodynamics literature documents, and it transitions when it crosses a basin boundary. The signature output is **distance-to-bifurcation** — how close a society is to qualitative reorganization — expressed always as a probability over a horizon, never as a date.

**Three ideas held as research hypotheses, not results.** The discipline is candid that its most ambitious theoretical moves are not yet established. *Reflexivity* — that a public model of social dynamics becomes part of the system it models — is treated as a coupling to be characterized, not a solved problem. Candidate *quasi-conservation laws* (for legitimacy, coercive capacity, information entropy) are **empirical hypotheses with stated falsification conditions, not consequences of Noether's theorem**: the discipline has no established action principle from which conservation laws could be derived, and prior framing that implied otherwise is corrected in Document 2 §8. The hypothesis of *universality classes* near transitions is exactly that — a hypothesis, testable by looking for scaling and data-collapse, not a property the framework may assume.

## 5. The measurement commitment

A theory of civilizational dynamics is only as serious as its measurement program, and the discipline binds itself to operational, reproducible, openly published measurement. Every index is to be published with its exact construction, its baseline and the defense of that baseline, the data vintage, the normalization, the uncertainty, and the code (Document 4 specifies this in full).

Two commitments deserve emphasis here because they are matters of integrity, not only of method. First, **estimated quantities are never to be presented as computed.** Much of the corpus today contains illustrative estimates; these must be labeled as such in every artifact, public or internal, with no computed-looking precision standing in for measurement that has not been done. Second, **the ECI is the highest-priority empirical investment of the program.** The information layer is the discipline's distinctive contribution, and it is also its least mature measure; credibility depends on *building* validated epistemic-coherence measurement — a standing factual-consensus instrument, cross-community media-overlap and network pipelines, and the best feasible historical reconstruction — rather than asserting ECI values.

On the substance of the information layer, the discipline advances specific **mechanisms**, stated as such and kept separate from stronger claims of coordinated intent that would be harder to establish and would pull toward unfalsifiability: that engagement-optimizing platforms systematically amplify emotionally arousing, identity-confirming, low-accuracy content, degrading the shared factual commons; that above a threshold of fragmentation populations undergo an **epistemic phase separation** into near-hermetic communities that diverge on basic factual premises, disabling the self-correction that democratic competition presupposes; that a **velocity mismatch** between institutional and informational timescales prevents correction from operating faster than destabilization; and that the dominant information infrastructure is **structurally misaligned** with social stability because its revenue depends on maximizing arousal. These are the engine of the epistemic-layer research program (Documents 4 and 5).

## 6. Validation architecture

This section is the heart of the discipline's claim to be a science. The standards below are binding and stated in advance.

**Retrodiction.** A minimum historical test set must be correctly characterized — meaning the framework must show elevated stress preceding these transitions — or the framework is falsified and must be revised or abandoned: the late Roman Republic, the Weimar Republic, the French Revolution, the Soviet collapse, the 1848 European revolutions, and the United States 2008–2024. The success bar is correct characterization in at least 80% of the set. The worked retrodictions live in **Document 6**, each tagged to the evidentiary tier the available data support (the distinction between locating attractors and reconstructing dynamics is drawn in Document 2 §3). Document 6 reads the bar as one of *discrimination* — the framework must also not flag high-stress periods that did not transition — and states the chance level of the test, which on the original set of cases is too high for a pass to count as validation (Document 6 §5.3).

**Prospective prediction.** All predictions are probability distributions over defined outcomes with explicit update conditions and resolution criteria, timestamped and published before the window opens, and scored with Brier scores upon resolution. The discipline targets calibration exceeding the best superforecasters on structural questions with three-to-ten-year horizons — a high bar, achievable only if the theory is sound. The public, auditable record is the mechanism of accountability (§9).

**Falsifiability conditions.** The core claims fail if: the stress index, constructed prospectively with pre-specified weights, systematically does not precede transitions in the test set; prospective predictions, tracked over a ten-year window, fail to beat a naive baseline on Brier score; cross-civilizational analysis reveals no common structural variables predicting transitions across the set; or the information/epistemic layer, controlled for economic and demographic variables, shows no independent contribution to disruption probability. Each is a way the discipline could be wrong, stated so that being wrong is possible.

## 7. Relationship to existing disciplines

Civilodynamics is integrative by design and collaborative by disposition. The table states what it borrows, where it departs, and where the most productive collaboration lies. The earlier "supersedes" framing has been retired in favor of "departs from," which is both more accurate and more conducive to the alliances the discipline needs.

|------------|---------|--------------|--------------------|

| Complexity science | Phase transitions, emergence, power laws, networks | Metaphorical rather than literal application to societies | Core mathematical language; SFI as institutional partner |
| Physics | Dynamical systems, statistical mechanics, universality (as hypothesis) | Not departed from — borrowed at the mathematical level | Non-equilibrium statistical mechanics; sociophysics |
| Superforecasting | Probabilistic calibration, Brier scoring, epistemic-humility norms | Atheoretical; short horizons | Calibration methodology; prediction-tournament infrastructure |
| Economics | Mathematical formalism, behavioral economics, game theory | Equilibrium assumptions; exogenous politics | Complexity economics; behavioral foundations |

## 8. The twenty-year arc

The detailed, dependency-aware, continuously updated program is **Document 8**; this is only its shape. The trajectory runs from foundational theory and a validated measurement framework, through the construction of historical and real-time databases and the first cross-civilizational validation studies, to a formal dynamical treatment with a public prediction platform, then to cross-civilizational testing and theoretical refinement, and finally to full institutionalization — a journal, a curriculum, an institute, and a self-reproducing community of researchers. The arc is calibrated against what comparable communities have achieved: the Santa Fe Institute turned complexity science from provocation into discipline in roughly two decades, and cliodynamics moved quantitative history from the fringe to a recognized program in a similar span. The governing principle across the arc is that **rigor leads reach** — priority is established early and cheaply, but public promotion of predictive claims waits until the measurement infrastructure is real.

## 9. Institutional architecture

The institutional vision is long-horizon and aspirational; its sequencing is in Document 8. It comprises four elements.

**The Institute**, modeled on the Santa Fe Institute: an independent, cross-disciplinary research center with no departmental structure to enforce disciplinary orthodoxy, whose permanent members span complex-systems physics, dynamical-systems mathematics, quantitative history, complexity and behavioral economics, and comparative social science, with no single discipline holding a majority. Funding is to be diversified across foundations, government research agencies, and international bodies to protect independence.

**The Journal of Civilodynamics**, publishing only work that is quantitative, falsifiable, and cross-scale, operating open-access with mandatory pre-registration for empirical studies.

**The Curriculum**, purpose-built across mathematics (dynamical systems, stochastic processes, networks, Bayesian inference), physics (statistical mechanics, phase-transition theory), empirical social science (quantitative history, comparative analysis, survey methodology), data science (pipelines, NLP, machine learning), and philosophy of science (falsificationism, the epistemology of complex systems, the ethics of predictive social science).

**The Public Prediction Platform**, which publishes every structural prediction with its claim, probability, the state-vector components it rests on, its update conditions, and its resolution criteria, and scores all of them with Brier scores on an openly accessible record. This is simultaneously the discipline's accountability mechanism and its strongest credibility instrument — precisely because it holds the field to a standard most fields avoid.

## 10. Ethical commitments

The ability to characterize civilizational states probabilistically creates real obligations. The discipline binds itself to four.

**Transparency.** Predictions are published openly so that their influence on outcomes — the reflexivity effect — is distributed across the population rather than concentrated in the hands of any actor positioned to benefit from or accelerate a predicted disruption.

**Funding integrity.** Civilodynamicists do not accept funding or employment from actors whose interests are served by the destabilization of the systems being modeled; the Institute is to maintain an ethics board with real authority over publication and funding.

**Dual-use care.** The discipline develops explicit protocols for findings that carry immediate crisis implications, analogous to those governing the publication of dual-use biological research.

**Epistemic honesty as ethics.** The commitments of §5 — never presenting estimated values as computed, demoting contested claims to operationalized and falsifiable sub-hypotheses, and publishing failures alongside successes — are treated not merely as methodology but as the ethical core of a predictive social science, the safeguard against a discipline that flatters its founders' priors or its funders' interests.

## 11. Founding statement

Human civilizations are the most complex dynamical systems we know of, exhibiting the full repertoire of complex-system behavior — self-organization, emergence, phase transitions, criticality, cascading failure — and they are the systems whose behavior most urgently demands understanding, because the consequences of their transitions are borne by billions. The existing sciences have produced deep knowledge of the parts. What has been missing is a discipline willing to commit to the whole: to treat the civilization as the unit of analysis, to model the coupling between its subsystems, to build the measurement infrastructure its state requires, and to hold itself to the falsifiability standards that separate science from sophisticated description.

Civilodynamics makes that commitment, with full awareness of the difficulty. The reflexivity problem is real, the measurement challenges are substantial, the formalism is partly undeveloped, and the historical data are imperfect. These are research problems, not reasons to abandon the work — and the alternative, continuing to analyze civilizational dynamics without a discipline designed to do so, has costs now measurable in historical events.

> *The goal is not to be Hari Seldon. The goal is to build the instrument that made Seldon possible — to develop the measurement theory, the mathematical formalism, and the institutional infrastructure that a predictive science of civilizational dynamics requires.*

The founding of this discipline begins here. The first commitments are stated, the first falsifiability conditions set, and the corrected foundations laid in the documents this charter governs. The clock starts now.
