# Civilodynamics — Retrodiction Casebook

**Canonical Corpus, Document 6 of 8 · Working Draft v0.1 · June 2026**
**Satchel Hamilton · Civilodynamics Initiative**

> **Status and role.** This document is the discipline's validation instrument: the set of historical cases against which the framework's core empirical claim — that elevated Civilizational Stress precedes phase transitions — is to be tested, against the charter's bar (Document 1 §6). The method is specified in **Document 2** and operationalized in **Document 4**; this document supplies the cases, the scoring design, and the pre-registered quantitative test.
>
> **What this document is, and is not.** It is **not** a set of computed results. The measurement pipeline (Document 4) has not been run on any historical case, and every qualitative assessment here is **illustrative and provisional** — and, crucially, is at risk of the very bias this document is designed to control. It **is** three things: (i) a retrodiction *protocol*; (ii) illustrative, tier-tagged structural sketches of each case showing what the framework claims and how each could fail; and (iii) a **pre-registration** of the quantitative test, recorded now, before computation, so that the eventual result cannot be fit to the cases after the fact. The pre-registered quantitative test, not the qualitative sketches, is the real arbiter.

---

## 1. The central hazard: retrodiction is where you fool yourself

Retrodiction has a structural weakness that this document must confront before anything else. The outcomes are already known. We know Rome's Republic became a Principate, that Weimar fell, that the Soviet Union dissolved. Knowing the outcome, it is trivial to assemble a structural narrative that appears to "predict" it — to find, after the fact, the stresses that "must have" driven a transition we already know occurred. A framework validated this way has demonstrated nothing except the analyst's ingenuity. This is the confirmation-bias and overfitting risk flagged in the program's risk register (Document 8) and is the reason the discipline commits to pre-registration (Document 2 §11.4).

The casebook is designed around four safeguards against this hazard, and the design is the document's backbone:

1. **Blind computation.** The stress trajectory for each case is to be computed from the structural data *without reference to the outcome*, by the disclosed pipeline of Document 4 — then checked against the outcome, not fit to it.
2. **Pre-registration.** The scoring criteria, the cases, and the quantitative test are fixed *now*, in this document, before computation. This document is itself the pre-registration record.
3. **Negative controls.** The set deliberately includes high-stress periods that did **not** produce a transition (§4). A framework that signals transition during every stressed period has zero specificity and predicts nothing; the negative controls are what make the test two-sided and therefore falsifiable.
4. **Two-sided scoring.** Success requires both *sensitivity* (stress elevated before the transitions) and *specificity* (stress not elevated, or a structural-correction signature present, during the non-transition controls). The charter's 80% bar is reinterpreted here as a bar on **discrimination**, not merely on hitting known transitions (§5).

## 2. The retrodiction protocol

For each case, the procedure is:

1. **Define the window and the unit.** State the society and the time window, resolving the unit-of-analysis question (Document 3 §7) explicitly for the case.
2. **Assemble structural data.** Populate the stress readout $\mathbf{u}(t)$ (and, where possible, the configuration vector $\mathbf{S}$) from the historical record, with sources and uncertainty (Document 4).
3. **Compute blind.** Run the Document 4 pipeline to produce a CSI trajectory with bands, without reference to the known outcome.
4. **Tag the tier.** Locating the society in poliform morphospace near the relevant boundary is **Tier 1** (estimable from structural data); reconstructing the *temporal approach* to the boundary requires a longitudinal trajectory and is **Tier 2** and data-intensive (Document 2 §3). Most historical CSI *trajectories* are therefore Tier-2-demanding and currently illustrative.
5. **Score against pre-registered criteria** (§5), on both sensitivity and specificity.
6. **Note in-sample contamination.** Cases used to *build* the parent structural-demographic theory are weaker tests than genuinely out-of-sample cases; this is flagged per case.

**Current status:** Step 3 has not been performed for any case. Sections 3–4 are illustrative sketches against which the eventual blind computation will be compared.

## 3. The positive cases (transitions)

Each case is sketched across the six stress blocks, tagged for tier and for in/out-of-sample status, with a per-case falsifier. The information-layer ($\sigma_{\mathrm{epi}}$) treatment is highlighted because it is the discipline's distinctive axis — and the cases demonstrate, importantly, that the information axis is **not** a modern-only construct: it appears in recognizable form across eras (the early-modern public sphere; the telegraph-and-newspaper acceleration of 1848; the Soviet information opening), while being honestly weak for antiquity.

### 3.1 The late Roman Republic (133–27 BCE)
*Structural sketch.* Elite overproduction (an expanding senatorial and equestrian aspirant class competing through a narrow *cursus honorum*); popular immiseration (displacement of smallholders by latifundia and slave labor; the Gracchan land crisis); legitimacy decline (erosion of *mos maiorum*, escalating political violence from the Gracchi through Sulla to the civil wars); a fiscal-military restructuring (Marian reforms producing client armies loyal to generals rather than the state). Information layer: weakly applicable — an oral and elite-literate environment; this block is the least informative for antiquity and the casebook says so.
*Transition.* Endogenous drift to autocracy (the Principate); a Type-1 cross-basin transition within the universalist-empire trajectory.
*In/out-of-sample.* **Near-in-sample** — Rome is central to the structural-demographic tradition this framework extends, so a "successful" retrodiction here is *weak* evidence; the framework was partly built with this case in view.
*Falsifier.* If a blind CSI computation does not show elevated, rising stress across the available blocks through the first century BCE, the case fails.

### 3.2 The French Revolution (1787–1799)
*Structural sketch.* Fiscal state failure (crown insolvency, war debt, inability to tax the privileged orders); elite fragmentation (robe vs. sword nobility; the Assembly of Notables' refusal; the Estates-General crisis); popular immiseration (the 1788 harvest failure, bread prices); legitimacy collapse (of monarchical and clerical authority). **Information layer (notable):** the explosion of pamphlets and the coffeehouse/salon public sphere is a credible proxy for an information-architecture shift in the early-modern context — the era's analog of an information-regime change.
*Transition.* Type-1 cross-basin (absolute monarchy → revolutionary regime → Napoleonic consolidation).
*In/out-of-sample.* Partially out-of-sample; a moderately strong test.
*Falsifier.* Failure to show rising fiscal, elite, and immiseration stress through the 1780s.

### 3.3 The Weimar Republic (1919–1933)
*Structural sketch.* Severe economic stress (the 1921–23 hyperinflation destroying savings; mass unemployment by 1932; the Brüning austerity); elite overproduction (post-war university expansion producing a frustrated credentialed cohort); legitimacy never consolidated (a parliament associated with defeat and catastrophe); epistemic fragmentation (a party press, including an explicitly propagandistic right-wing tabloid sector, with no shared factual commons); elite fragmentation (traditional conservative elites abandoning democratic parties).
*Transition.* Type-1 collapse of the democratic configuration.
*In/out-of-sample.* Out-of-sample for the framework's quantitative pipeline; a strong test. *(Weimar also appears in Document 5 as the U.S. comparison; there it is hypothesis-generating analogy, here it is a formal retrodiction case — distinct roles.)*
*Falsifier.* Failure to show elevated stress across economic, elite, and legitimacy blocks through 1928–1932.

### 3.4 The Soviet collapse (1985–1991)
*Structural sketch.* State fiscal failure (stagnating growth, the 1986 oil-price collapse, military overextension including Afghanistan); legitimacy collapse (eroded ideological belief; Chernobyl as a legitimacy shock); elite defection (the nomenklatura and republican elites abandoning the center). **Information layer (notable and instructive):** *glasnost* — a deliberate *opening* of the information environment — accelerated legitimacy collapse by revealing systemic failures. This is an illuminating near-inverse of the algorithmic-fragmentation story: in this case loosening information control precipitated transition, underscoring that the information axis matters in both directions.
*Transition.* Rapid Type-1/Type-3 loss of stability once elite defection cascaded.
*In/out-of-sample.* Largely out-of-sample; one of the stronger tests, and valuable for its rapid, information-mediated dynamics.
*Falsifier.* Failure to show a sharp rise in fiscal and legitimacy stress and an elite-defection signature across the late 1980s.

### 3.5 The 1848 European revolutions
*Structural sketch and special role.* The charter designates 1848 as a **synchronization** test: multiple societies approaching transition near-simultaneously, which — if structural stress drives transitions — should produce *correlated* transitions under correlated stress. The 1840s "hungry forties" (harvest failures, food prices) combined with a wave of liberal-national elite aspiration meeting closed political systems. **Information layer (notable):** cheap newspapers, the telegraph, and railways accelerated the cross-border spread of revolutionary news — a genuine information-velocity effect well before the digital era.
*Transition.* A wave of Type-1 transitions, **mostly reversed** within a few years — which makes 1848 doubly valuable: it tests both synchronization and the framework's ability to handle *partial and reversed* transitions rather than only terminal ones.
*In/out-of-sample.* Out-of-sample; a strong, distinctive test.
*Falsifier.* If correlated stress across these societies does **not** precede the correlated 1848 transitions, the synchronization claim fails.

### 3.6 The United States, 2008–2024
Treated in full in **Document 5**. *In/out-of-sample:* **near-in-sample** (the framework was developed partly in view of this case), hence — like Rome — a *weak* test on its own; its value lies in the prospective predictions (Document 5 §7), which are genuinely out-of-sample.

## 4. The negative controls — the discrimination test

This is the most important section for the framework's scientific status, and the one most often missing from work of this kind. Without periods of high stress that did *not* transition, "elevated stress precedes transitions" is unfalsifiable. The set therefore includes controls that the framework must distinguish from the positive cases.

### 4.1 The U.S. Gilded Age → Progressive Era (≈1890–1920) — high stress that resolved
*Why it is a control.* The late-19th-century U.S. exhibited high inequality, elite overproduction, agrarian and labor immiseration, and a genuine anti-systemic surge (the Populist movement; the 1896 election). Yet the system did **not** undergo a terminal transition; it **reformed** — antitrust, progressive taxation, the regulatory and administrative state — and stress subsided. *What the framework must show:* either a CSI that, while elevated, did not reach the levels of the positive cases, **or** a detectable structural-correction signature (the reforms reversing the stress drivers). This control bears directly on Document 5's Prediction 5 — the possibility that a structural correction pulls a society back from a boundary — and a framework that cannot distinguish "stress that resolved via reform" from "stress that transitioned" has not earned its predictive claims.

### 4.2 Britain in the 1830s–1840s vs. the Continent in 1848 — the release valve
*Why it is a control.* Britain faced Chartism and structural pressures comparable to the continental states that revolted in 1848, yet experienced no 1848-style revolution — plausibly because incremental enfranchisement (the 1832 Reform Act and subsequent reforms) acted as a pressure-release valve. *What the framework must show:* a divergence in the structural trajectory — Britain's stress relieved or its boundary distance maintained — relative to the revolting states. This is a paired natural experiment: same era, similar pressures, different structural-release conditions, different outcomes.

### 4.3 The mid-20th-century U.S. baseline (≈1948–1973) — the low-stress anchor
*Why it is a control.* By construction this is the U.S. stable-baseline period, and the framework must show low CSI here — a basic sanity check that the index is anchored correctly. (That this period also contained serious injustices the baseline does not capture is acknowledged in Documents 4 and 5; it is a stress *baseline*, not a normative ideal.)

### 5.2 The pre-registered quantitative test (recorded June 2026, before computation)

- **Computation is blind to outcome**, by the Document 4 pipeline, for all nine cases.
- **Discrimination metric.** Treating "transition within a defined horizon" as the target, compute the discriminative performance of the CSI (e.g., the area under the ROC curve, AUC, for CSI-at-time-$t$ predicting transition-within-horizon across positive and control cases), with a **pre-specified target threshold** to be fixed in the computation plan and not adjusted afterward.
- **Two-sided bar.** The charter's 80% standard is interpreted as a bar on *discrimination*: the framework must both flag the positive cases (sensitivity) **and** not flag the controls (specificity). Hitting the transitions while also flagging the controls is a failure, not a success.
- **Publication commitment.** The result is published whether or not it confirms the framework, with the blind computations and code released (Document 4 §9). A failed retrodiction set falsifies the core empirical claim (charter §6) and is reported as such.

## 6. Safeguards against confirmation bias (consolidated)

Restated as a checklist the program binds itself to: blind computation; pre-registration (this document); negative controls and two-sided scoring; adversarial review by collaborators not invested in the framework's success (Document 8, Stage C1); and publication of failures alongside successes. These are not optional rigor; they are the difference between a science and a collection of just-so stories about events we already know the endings of.

## 7. What this casebook is not

It is not validation. As of this draft, no case has been computed, and the qualitative sketches of §3–4 are provisional and subject to exactly the bias §1 warns against. The casebook's value now is as a *protocol and a pre-registration*: it fixes the cases, the controls, the scoring, and the bar before the data can talk back. When the computations are run, this document is the standard they are held to.

## 8. Closing

The Retrodiction Casebook is the instrument that will either earn the framework its empirical credibility or take it away. Its design reflects the recognition that retrodicting known outcomes is easy and therefore nearly worthless unless it is done blind, pre-registered, and — above all — tested against negative controls that the framework could fail. The positive cases (Rome, the French Revolution, Weimar, the Soviet collapse, 1848, the United States) ask whether elevated stress precedes transition; the negative controls (the Progressive-Era correction, the British release valve, the mid-century baseline) ask the harder and more important question of whether the framework can tell the difference between a society that transitions and one that is merely stressed. A framework that passes the first test and fails the second has predicted nothing. The pre-registered quantitative test of §5 is where that verdict will be rendered.
